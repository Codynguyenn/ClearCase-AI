## Post-hoc, not pre-registered: the malformed phone numbers

A second flaw in the synthetic data, seen only after the run in the per-category table above: the generator wrote every phone number with a 3-digit last group (for example `555-044`), so each has 9 digits once the country code is dropped and is **not a valid US phone number**. The pattern detectors validate numbers, so they cannot match any of the 18 phone numbers in this set; their PHONE recall of 0 is an artefact of the data, not a detector result (the models found the numbers and called them truncated). The headline keeps the frozen data. Here those items are neither required nor penalised, alone and together with the company addresses. Decided after seeing the results: an explanation of the headline numbers, not a replacement for them.

## Limits of this evaluation

12 short synthetic documents written by one author (who also wrote the policies and the prompt); one language; one run per document and model, so run-to-run variance is not measured; three injected documents is a demonstration, not a rate; the keep rule covers company names only; the labels call every address sensitive although 8 lines belong to companies (see the post-hoc section); the task is easy (one-page documents, clean text), so it cannot tell strong models apart; prices and model ids change; ground truth is the author's definition of "personal information". This says nothing about real documents.
